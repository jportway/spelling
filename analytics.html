Sign in to read the log

Or open a file instead — drop one here, or use the button above. Useful offline, or for a snapshot kept to one side.

python3 tools/fetch_logs.py --out log.json pulls the same data down with a gcloud or service-account credential.

python3 tools/simulate_logs.py --out fake.json invents a term of practice with a known learning curve in it, for seeing the shape of the thing before there is enough real data to say much.

How to read this

      

        

          

            Only first attempts count. When a letter goes in
            the wrong hole the tile falls back to the pile and she tries
            again — but now she knows one letter it is not. Counting that
            second guess alongside the first would quietly flatter her, so
            every accuracy figure here is built from her first attempt at each
            hole.
          

          

            Every rate carries its count. A percentage from
            eleven attempts is barely information. The ranges shown are 95%
            intervals: the range the true figure plausibly sits in given how
            few attempts there have been. Two letters whose ranges overlap
            have not been shown to differ, however far apart their percentages
            look.
          

          

            The game is part of the measurement. She can only
            put a “d” where a “b” belongs if a “d” was on the table. The puzzle
            generator offers the confusable partner about nine times in ten
            when the missing letter is tricky, so raw swap counts are
            conditional on an offer that is not always made. Where the pool was
            recorded, the rates are worked out over the times the wrong letter
            was actually there to pick.
          

          

            Before and after splits on attempts, not dates.
            Half her practice on each side, whenever it happened. Splitting on
            the calendar would put a fortnight's holiday in one bucket and
            three rounds in the other, then report the difference as progress.
          

          

            Six questions at once is not one question. At the
            usual one-in-twenty threshold, showing a verdict for each of six
            letters means something gets flagged as “measurably worse” almost
            every time you look, purely by chance — and that is exactly the
            false alarm somebody would act on. So the per-letter and per-swap
            verdicts are held to a bar divided by how many are being asked
            together. The single figure for her overall accuracy is one
            pre-specified question and keeps the ordinary threshold.
          

          

            “No detectable change” is not “no change”. It
            means the data cannot tell the difference yet. With a few rounds a
            week that is usually the honest answer for months, which is why
            the summary says how many more attempts a given question would
            need.
          

          

            Times include the word being read out. Most words
            have one hole and she gets most of them first go, so nearly every
            timing is the opening move — which waits on the speech. That puts
            a floor of a couple of seconds under all of it. The trend still
            means something, because the speech costs the same every round.
